Appendix A  ·  Design reasoning

Portfolio appearance & design

Palette, and why these colours

Black and gold on screen, tan and bronze in the hand, for the reasons set out in section 12. This document uses the digital palette because it is read on a screen, with the body text lifted well clear of the ground: a website can afford dim cream on ink because the reader is skimming, and a document of this length cannot.

Why the site and this document read in different faces

The three house faces are set out in section 12. One thing about them belongs here rather than there, because it is a decision about this document rather than about the brand: the reading face is not the same in both places. They are read differently. This is sixty-nine pages of continuous prose, which is what EB Garamond was revived to carry: sturdy, even in colour, and undemanding over a long sitting. The website is read in short passages between images, where a finer and more contrasted face can be afforded, so it runs on Cormorant Garamond instead: the same old-style skeleton drawn with higher contrast and a smaller eye. It looks better for being afforded, and it would tire the eye long before page sixty-nine.

The pairing is the same in both, and so is everything above it: Cinzel for every heading, label and numeral, Playfair italic for the flourish. Only the reading face changes, and it changes because the reading changes.

The classical serif wordmark and the script Perfumes pairing echo the brand's Italian, Roman-influenced aesthetic without leaning on cliché. The bust mark and the Greek meander border repeat quietly across every section, never shouting, always present, because exclusivity is communicated through consistency and restraint, not volume.

The website is published in three languages

English, Arabic and Italian, switchable from the navigation. Arabic required its own typography rather than the house faces, since Cinzel, Playfair Display and EB Garamond carry no Arabic glyphs at all: Reem Kufi for display, because Kufi is the Arabic script cut for stone exactly as Cinzel is the Latin one; Amiri for reading, a revival of the Bulaq naskh and the nearest Arabic has to an old-style book face; Aref Ruqaa for the flourish.

Organisation and navigation

Seventeen numbered sections, indexed in a fixed rail on every screen and in a full contents overlay, so the reader always knows both where they are and what else exists. The numbering is Roman in all three languages, because it is structure rather than language.
